# Case Study: Phishing Attack

Phishing is one of the most common types of cybercrime where attackers try to trick people into giving their personal or financial information. Instead of hacking directly, they act like a trusted person or organization to gain the victim’s trust.

For example, a student might receive an email saying they have been selected for a paid internship or that their bank account needs urgent verification. The message usually creates panic or excitement so that the person reacts quickly. It also contains a link that looks genuine. When the student clicks on it, they are taken to a fake website that looks exactly like the real one. Without realizing, they enter their details such as passwords or bank information, which directly goes to the attacker.

This process usually involves sending fake messages, creating realistic-looking websites, and using urgency to pressure the user. Students are often targeted because they frequently check emails for opportunities and may not always verify the source carefully.

The consequences can be serious. A person may lose money, their accounts can get hacked, or their personal identity can be misused. It can also affect their confidence in using online platforms.

This case shows that being aware is not enough. We need to stay careful, think before clicking, and always verify any suspicious message to avoid such situations.
