Digital literacy is all about having the skills and know-how to make the most of digital tech in your everyday life. It’s not just about knowing how to click around; it’s about finding, checking, creating, and sharing information online in a way that’s safe, responsible, and effective. When you’re digitally literate, you can confidently explore the internet, use various software tools, and think critically about whether what you’re seeing online is accurate and trustworthy.
A big part of being digitally literate is staying safe online. This means practicing good habits like coming up with strong, unique passwords, turning on two-factor authentication (2FA) wherever you can, being smart about spotting phishing scams, using secure websites, and keeping your devices and software up to date. These simple steps go a long way in protecting your personal information.
On top of safety, digital literacy is also about building a professional presence online. Think of it as your digital footprint—the image you project to the world. It’s about sharing content that's appropriate, highlighting your skills and what you've achieved, and making sure your online reputation is positive and credible. After all, employers, clients, and colleagues can all see what you put out there, so it pays to be thoughtful about how you show up in the digital world.
